The constraint is pace, not distance.

22:00 for 100K is 21:17/mi elapsed. A 100-miler with a standard 30-hour cutoff requires 18:00/mi — a faster pace than the 100K, sustained for 38 additional miles.

Three progressions follow from that: repeat 100K on faster terrain, hold 100K and add vertical, or run 100 miles on a course whose cutoff accommodates a 21-minute mile. Races for each are grouped further down.

How to read the cards

The ridgeline is a signature, not a course profile: height encodes feet of climb per mile, jaggedness encodes technicality, and the dashed line marks the Vermont 100K's climb rate (~172 ft/mi). Peaks above the dash indicate steeper average terrain. Grit is a 0–100 index weighting vert-per-mile (42%), technical footing (22%), altitude (20%) and distance (16%). Vermont 100K = 43. Mid-50s is a meaningful step up; 65+ changes the training required.
